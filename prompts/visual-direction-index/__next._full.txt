23:T993,<p>Use these shortcuts as a shared vocabulary. A shortcut is the start of a brief, not the complete prompt. Always add the subject, audience, aspect ratio, required labels, and what must remain technically accurate.</p>
<h2>Explain structure</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>/blueprint</code>: orthographic engineering drawing with measured callouts and restrained line work.</li>
<li><code>/layer</code>: stacked layers that explain how a product or interface is assembled.</li>
<li><code>/explodeview</code>: separated components, aligned to their assembly order.</li>
<li><code>/machineview</code>: one clear technical perspective focused on functional parts.</li>
<li><code>/machineview360</code>: a consistent multi-angle rotation sheet.</li>
<li><code>/topview</code>: a strict overhead view for layouts, boards, and workspaces.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Explain change</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>/sequence</code>: numbered frames showing a process in chronological order.</li>
<li><code>/beforeafter</code>: two matched frames with only the relevant change highlighted.</li>
<li><code>/defectview</code>: a diagnostic view that locates a fault, symptom, and likely cause.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Present an idea</h2>
<ul>
<li><code>/handwritten</code>: annotated notebook treatment for teaching and early reasoning.</li>
<li><code>/3dbillboard</code>: bold dimensional headline or object on a simple campaign stage.</li>
<li><code>/floating3d</code>: isolated components arranged in depth for a cover image.</li>
<li><code>/magazinecover</code>: editorial hierarchy with a strong title and controlled secondary copy.</li>
<li><code>/metaad</code>: social advertisement composition with one promise and one action.</li>
<li><code>/adcreativecode</code>: product-led ad concept where code or output is the main evidence.</li>
<li><code>/goldenhour</code>: warm natural light for human or field-project photography.</li>
<li><code>/wideshot</code>: environmental context that shows where a system operates.</li>
<li><code>/footwaretechpac</code>: manufacturing specification sheet. Use only for apparel or footwear work.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Portfolio cover recipe</h2>
<p>Start with one shortcut, then add: the real tool name, the problem it solves, one visible input-to-output relationship, the ASL ink and gold palette, Archivo and Space Mono labels, flat lighting, no glossy sci-fi effects, no fake UI, and no unreadable text. Keep the image useful at both 16:9 and mobile card crops.</p>

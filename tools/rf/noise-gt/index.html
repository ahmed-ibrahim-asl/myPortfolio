Understand the system

Thermal noise and bandwidth

Available thermal noise is kTB. Noise density is kT, independent of chosen bandwidth. The final’s 27°C means 300.15 K, giving about −129.06 dBW or −99.06 dBm over 30 MHz. A factor-ten bandwidth increase adds 10 dB noise.

N=kTB,N0=kTN=kTB,\quad N_0=kTN=kTB,N0​=kT

k: J/K; T: equivalent system K at stated reference plane; B: noise bandwidth in Hz; N: W; N_0: W/Hz.

Do not substitute Celsius in kTB.

Source: finalExam.pdf · PDF page 4

Common mistakes

  • Distinguish standard T₀, physical temperature and equivalent added temperature.
  • Linear gain 20 is 13.01 dB, not 20 dB.
  • dBm equals dBW + 30.

Worked approach

kTB and noise density

Noise factor is input SNR divided by output SNR. Noise figure is its dB representation. Equivalent added temperature is referred to the device input using the standard reference T₀ = 290 K. This reference is different from a physical source temperature of 300.15 K.

Understand the system

Physical versus effective aperture

A circular dish has projected area πD²/4. Effective aperture is efficiency times physical area; it represents the power captured from incident flux. At 10 GHz, 2 m and 70% efficiency, area is 3.1416 m² and effective area 2.1991 m².

A=πD2/4,Ae=ηA,G=4πAe/λ2A=\pi D^2/4,\quad A_e=\eta A,\quad G=4\pi A_e/\lambda^2A=πD2/4,Ae​=ηA,G=4πAe​/λ2

D, λ: m; A, A_e: m²; η: fractional efficiency; G: linear power gain.

A physical aperture is an area, not a dimensionless gain.

Source: finalExam.pdf · PDF page 4

Common mistakes

  • Use power gain 10 log, not voltage gain 20 log.
  • Efficiency is 0.70, not 70.
  • Area in dB needs the explicit reference 1 m².
Formula reference and symbol definitions

Physical aperture

A=πD2/4A=\pi D^2/4A=πD2/4

A: m²; D: m

Circular projected aperture.

Example: For D = 2 m, A = π × 2²/4 = π ≈ 3.142 m².

Aperture gain

G=4πAe/λ2G=4\pi A_e/\lambda^2G=4πAe​/λ2

G: linear; λ: m

Matched polarization and reciprocal antenna.

Example: For Aₑ = 1 m² and λ = 0.1 m, G = 4π/0.01 ≈ 1256.64 (linear).

Parabolic gain

G=η(πD/λ)2G=\eta(\pi D/\lambda)^2G=η(πD/λ)2

D,λ: m; η: fraction

Far-field dish approximation.

Example: For D = 2 m, λ = 0.03 m and η = 0.70, G = 0.70(π × 2/0.03)² ≈ 30705.4.

Worked approach

Wavelength

For the final preset, λ = 0.03 m and gain ≈ 44.87 dBi. dBi compares power gain to an isotropic reference. Doubling dish diameter quadruples area and adds approximately 6.02 dB gain at fixed frequency and efficiency.

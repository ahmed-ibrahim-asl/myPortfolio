Understand the system

Radius and circular motion

Orbit equations use distance from Earth’s center, not height above the surface. For the final’s 800 km altitude, 6371 km Earth radius and μ = 398600 km³/s², radius is 7171 km, speed about 7.46 km/s and period 6043.39 s. The printed 6030 s is inconsistent with those constants.

r=RE+h,v=μ/r,T=2πr3/μr=R_E+h,\quad v=\sqrt{\mu/r},\quad T=2\pi\sqrt{r^3/\mu}r=RE​+h,v=μ/r​,T=2πr3/μ​

r, R_E, h: km; μ: km³/s²; v: km/s; T: s.

Increasing radius lowers circular speed and increases period.

Source: finalExam.pdf · PDF page 2

Common mistakes

  • Never substitute altitude for orbital radius.
  • Apogee radius must not be smaller than perigee radius.
  • GSO period alone does not make an orbit geostationary.

Worked approach

Circular and elliptical orbits

The ideal orbit is an ellipse with Earth at one focus. Equal swept areas in equal times imply faster motion near perigee and slower motion near apogee. Period squared is proportional to semi-major axis cubed under the two-body, unperturbed model.

Questions engineers ask

Which topics does Orbit & Kepler Calculator cover?

Circular and elliptical orbits; Kepler laws; LEO, MEO, GEO, GSO and HEO; Polar and sun-synchronous orbits; Coverage.

How should I check my work in Orbit & Kepler Calculator?

Read the stated assumptions and source references, check units and independently repeat a worked example before relying on the result.

Does Orbit & Kepler Calculator operate real satellite equipment?

No. This is a browser-based educational resource. It does not transmit RF signals, command spacecraft or certify a working link.

Understand the system

Letter bands and service allocations

Approximate teaching ranges are L 1–2 GHz, S 2–4, C 4–8, X 8–12, Ku 12–18, K 18–26.5 and Ka 26.5–40 GHz. Ippolito rounds the K/Ka boundary to 27 GHz. Letter bands are broad labels: an actual service uses allocated subranges that depend on region and direction.

Source: week 3/reference.pdf · PDF page 33

Common mistakes

  • A letter band is not a precise licensed service allocation.
  • Do not teach that Ku waves travel faster than C waves.
  • Convert GHz to Hz before using c/f.

Worked approach

L, S, C, X, Ku, K and Ka bands

L supports mobile and navigation services. S includes MSS and space research examples. C is associated with FSS; X includes military and space applications. Ku serves satellite television, BSS and FSS. Ka supports broadband FSS/BSS. C-band links generally tolerate rain better than Ku/Ka; compare equal availability targets rather than assuming one band always wins.

Common decisions

Questions engineers ask

Which topics does RF Band & Carrier Planner cover?

L, S, C, X, Ku, K and Ka bands; FSS, MSS and BSS; Wavelength; Rain sensitivity.

How should I check my work in RF Band & Carrier Planner?

Read the stated assumptions and source references, check units and independently repeat a worked example before relying on the result.

Does RF Band & Carrier Planner operate real satellite equipment?

No. This is a browser-based educational resource. It does not transmit RF signals, command spacecraft or certify a working link.
